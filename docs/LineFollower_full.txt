Write a Fusion Python script that, when run, builds a complete two-wheel line-follower robot as a real assembly:
every part that would be bought or made, every screw and nut that holds them together, every hole those screws pass
through. It stands on a test board with a black line, ready to drive with Drive Joints.

INPUTS (constants at the top of the script, in mm unless said; x forwards, y left, z up):
- chassis: aluminium plate 3 thick, 160 long and 120 wide; rear corners rounded R 10; the front edge one arc through
  the two front corners that bulges 20 forwards; every edge of the outline chamfered 0.5
- gear motors (N20 type), two: a gearbox block 12 (along y) x 10 (along x) x 10 (z), a motor can of diameter 12 with
  two flats 10 apart (top and bottom), 15 long, behind the gearbox along y, and a D-shaft of diameter 3, 10 long, with
  a 0.5 deep flat, coming out of the gearbox face, pointing outwards along y
- motor brackets, two: a steel strap 1.5 thick and 10 wide (along y), bent into a U that hugs the gearbox's top and
  sides and ends in two feet that lie flat against the chassis underside; each foot screwed to the chassis with one M3
  socket head cap screw from above and an M3 hex nut below the foot
- wheels, two: a white plastic hub of diameter 34 and width 16, with 6 round lightening holes of diameter 7 on a circle
  of diameter 21, a boss of diameter 10 and 6 long on its inner face with a D-bore for the shaft, and an M3 set screw
  (grub screw, 4 long) in the boss, radial, bearing on the shaft's flat; a black rubber tyre, outer diameter 43, inner
  34, width 19, with 24 tread grooves 1.5 wide and 1 deep across its tread
- ball caster: a steel ball of diameter 9.5 held in a black plastic housing (a cylinder of diameter 14 open at the
  bottom) with a flange 30 x 10 x 2 carrying two holes for M3 screws 22 apart; between the flange and the chassis, two
  aluminium round spacers (outer 6, bore 3.2) as long as needed; two M3 cap screws from above through the chassis and
  spacers, an M3 hex nut under the flange on each
- sensor bar: a green circuit board 72 x 14 x 1.6 carrying 5 reflective sensors (TCRT5000, each a black block
  10.2 x 5.8 x 7) spaced 14 along y, facing the ground; the board held by two aluminium L-brackets (1.5 thick, 10
  wide), each screwed to the chassis underside with one M3 screw and nut, and to the board with one M3 screw and nut
- controller: a blue circuit board 60 x 40 x 1.6 (an Arduino Nano carrier) with an Arduino Nano on it (45 x 18 x 1.6
  board, a USB socket block 8 x 7.5 x 4 at its rear end, two rows of 15 header pins of 0.64 square, 2.54 pitch, 8
  tall); the carrier on four brass hex standoffs M3, 10 long, 5.5 across the flats; one M3 cap screw into each
  standoff from above the carrier and one from below the chassis
- battery: a black holder 77 x 41 x 20 for two 18650 cells (each diameter 18, length 65, as two bodies, modelled in
  Aluminum: a real cell weighs about 46 g), fixed with two M3 countersunk screws from below through the chassis and an
  M3 nut on each inside the holder
- fasteners, drawn as they are bought: M3 socket head cap screw (ISO 4762): head diameter 5.5, head height 3, a
  hexagon socket 2.5 across the flats and 1.5 deep, shank diameter 3; M3 countersunk screw (ISO 10642): head diameter
  6, 90 degrees; M3 hex nut (ISO 4032): 5.5 across the flats, 2.4 thick, bore 3; every screw length a standard one
  (6, 8, 10, 12, 16, 20) chosen so that at least 1 thread pitch (0.5) shows past its nut
- holes: every screw passes through a clearance hole of diameter 3.4 in every plate, foot, flange and board it crosses
- test board: white, 600 x 400 x 5, its top face is the ground, z = 0; a black line 19 wide, set 0.5 deep into it
  (cut the groove, fill it with the line): a straight 200 long, then a quarter circle of radius 120

DERIVED (computed in the script, never typed):
- wheel axle height = tyre radius
- chassis underside height = axle height + half the gearbox height + the bracket's 1.5 (the bracket sits between)
- the caster spacers' length, so that the ball touches the ground
- the sensor brackets' drop, so that the sensors' bottom faces are 4 above the ground
- the wheels' y position: the tyre's inner face 3 clear of the chassis side; the shaft long enough to reach through
  the hub's boss
- each screw's length, as stated above

HARD CONSTRAINTS:
- No part may be inside another, except: each motor's shaft in its wheel's D-bore, each set screw against its flat,
  the cells in their holder's cavities, and screws in standoffs or nuts (their bores are 3, the screws 3).

- The robot stands at the start of the black line, its sensor bar centred over it.
- Every material set and printed back as Fusion gave it: Aluminum, Steel, Brass, ABS Plastic, Rubber, Silicone, and
  FR4 for the boards if it exists. Colour every part as described ("Fusion Appearance Library").

FUSION CONVENTIONS:
- Create a new Fusion design document (FusionDesignDocumentType). The API works in cm and radians: convert.
- One component per part, named: Chassis, Motor L, Motor R, Motor bracket L, Motor bracket R, Wheel L, Wheel R,

  Sensor bracket L, Sensor bracket R, Carrier, Arduino Nano, Standoff 1 ... Standoff 4, Battery holder, Cell 1,

- The script has run(context) and a function build() that builds everything and returns a one-line message; run()

ACCEPTANCE (the check prints ok or FAIL for each):

- at least 20 screws and 14 nuts
